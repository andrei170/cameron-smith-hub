Wedge

Productised ladder honest to a Gen Z + young millennial audience. Sponsorships co-exist as the upper layer; the ladder pulls a separate revenue stream from the same audience.

  1. Free lead magnet - "ISA + Tax Allowance Audit" interactive scorecard (12 questions, returns 0-100 score, segments by life stage: student / first job / first home / first investor / first landlord). Captures email.
  2. Low-tier: £47-£97 self-paced "Money School" video course. Covers the foundational system Cazza already teaches in fragments: budgeting, ISA stack, tax basics, side-hustle tax, first investment account.
  3. Mid-tier: £197-£497 "Cazza Time Cohort" - 6-week guided programme. Weekly live group Q&A with Cameron, action plans, accountability, capped intake size to keep the live element real.
  4. Newsletter / email nurture - Substack or Beehiiv tied to scorecard signup. The piece that turns 552K rented viewers into a long-term audience he owns.

Pricing is honest to the audience demographics. Cazza's audience is 18-25-year-olds learning their first ISA; £2K+ entry is the wrong door. Mid-ticket cohort + low-cost course is the honest fit. A £1.5K-£3K mentorship for the small slice of his audience who are landlords / freelancers / six-figure earners can sit above the cohort as a separate upmarket line later.

Estimated lift: £150-400K/yr Year-1 floor on top of existing sponsorship revenue, from cohort + course + community + newsletter monetisation.

Sensitivities

  • Gen Z ticket sensitivity: A £997-£3K mentorship to a 22-year-old learning their first ISA is the wrong product. Default the entry product to £47-£97 (self-paced) or £197-£497 (cohort). High-ticket mentorship belongs as an upmarket line aimed at the slice of his audience who are landlords, freelancers, or six-figure earners.
  • UK FCA regulatory boundary: Cameron is a content creator, not an authorised financial advisor. Course copy must stay in "education and information" framing - "here's how ISAs work" not "buy this fund." Disclaimer baked into landing.
  • Sponsor revenue is the cash flow: Don't recommend offers that conflict with HSBC / Experian / XTB-tier sponsors. The cohort and community sit above sponsor work; they don't replace it.
  • Bank of England + Global Fraud Summit credibility is the moat: Don't dilute it with low-rent "make money fast" copy. Course copy stays in the educator-tone register. He is not a "trader bro" and the audience would notice immediately.
  • Engineering brain, not influencer brain: Voice is precise, technical, calm. Course copy mirrors that register. Numbers must be defensible - he will spot inflated math instantly.
  • UK-only audience: Stay native to ISAs, SIPPs, NI, student loan plans 1/2/4/5. No US framing ("401k", "Roth").
