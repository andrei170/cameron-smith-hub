Email 2 - Sent 24 hours after Email 1

Subject: The ISA most UK Gen Z get wrong

Most UK Gen Z under 30 have one ISA. They opened it because someone on TikTok said to. They don't know which type they have. They don't know what the limit is. They don't know that the allowance resets on the 5th of April every year.

Quick fix.

You've got £20,000 of tax-free ISA allowance per tax year. It splits across four types - Cash, Stocks & Shares, Lifetime, Innovative Finance. The right one for you depends on your timeline.

If you need the money inside 12 months, Cash ISA. Boring, safe, 4-5% if you shop around.

If your timeline is 5+ years, Stocks & Shares ISA. The maths beats Cash over any 5-year window in modern UK history.

If you're saving for a first home and you're under 40, Lifetime ISA. Government adds 25% on top of every pound you put in, up to £4K/year. £1K free per year. The closest thing to free money the UK government will ever give you.

Email 4 - Sent 7 days after Email 1

Subject: The student loan thing nobody explains right

Plan 1, Plan 2, Plan 4, Plan 5 - which plan you're on changes the entire calculation.

If you are on Plan 2 (most English / Welsh grads since 2012), the loan writes off after 30 years. The interest rate is RPI + up to 3%. For most graduates earning £30K-£60K, the loan behaves like a graduate tax, not a debt. Paying it off early often loses you money.

If you are on Plan 5 (anyone starting uni from September 2023), the loan writes off after 40 years and the threshold is lower. The maths flips. Paying down faster sometimes makes sense.

Most UK Gen Z get told "pay off all debt" by US-style finance creators. That advice often costs you tens of thousands when applied to UK student loans.

If you want the full breakdown applied to your exact plan and earnings, that is week 3 of the cohort. Money School covers the same module self-paced if cohort timing does not work.
