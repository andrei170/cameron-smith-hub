Foundation

Copy Strategy - Cameron 'Cazza' Smith

Built: 2026-05-02 Built for: @cazza_time

Voice

  • Plain-English, no City-trader bravado, no jargon-flex
  • Slightly self-deprecating ("I had a Masters in Engineering and I still didn't know what an ISA was")
  • Engineer brain - numbers must check out, ranges given as floors not ceilings
  • UK-native vocabulary - ISA, SIPP, NI, tax code, plan 1/2/4/5 student loan, "uni" not "college"
  • Anti-guru. Pro-teacher.
  • Conversational register. Reads like a TikTok caption stretched into a paragraph, not a Wall Street whitepaper.

Brand pillars (mentorship landing copy hangs on these)

  1. Built by an engineer who learned the hard way. First-Class Masters, JLR / telecoms grad scheme, six months out from his first payslip he still couldn't read it. Started teaching himself. Started teaching everyone else.
  2. UK-native by design. ISAs, NI, tax codes, student loans. The money lessons school in the UK forgot. No US copy-paste.
  3. Trusted by the institutions. Bank of England guest. Global Fraud Summit speaker. No10 / Treasury invites. The same Cazza explaining your ISA on TikTok is the Cazza the Bank of England put in a room with their interest-rate experts.
  4. Build it once, run it for life. The mentorship isn't a get-rich pitch. It's the budget / save / invest operating system Cazza already runs - taught in 8 weeks, run forever.

Headline bank (mentorship landing)

  • "School never taught us how money works. So I'm teaching it."
  • "The 8-week UK money operating system - built by the engineer the Bank of England invited in."
  • "From your first payslip to your first ISA to your first £100K. The full UK Gen Z stack."
  • "I had a First-Class Masters and still couldn't read my payslip. Here's the system I wish I had on day one."
  • "If you're under 30, in the UK, and want your money to actually work - this is the cohort."
  • "8 weeks. One Zoom a week. The full UK money playbook."

Subhead bank

  • "Built for UK Gen Z professionals - first job, first payslip, first ISA, first £100K."
  • "Plain-English. Engineer-grade. Bank of England trusted."
  • "Less jargon than your bank's app. More math than your favourite finance TikTok."

Sub-CTA microcopy

  • "Apply to the cohort"
  • "Skip the setter call - apply direct"
  • "Join the next intake"
  • "Reserve a seat (cohorts cap at 150)"

Body copy register

  • Short paragraphs. 2-4 sentences max.
  • One number per claim.
  • Ranges given as floors. "From X to Y" not "up to Z."
  • Personal pronouns. "You" addressing one specific UK Gen Z reader. "I" when Cazza speaks.
  • Zero em dashes (use commas, full stops, parentheses instead).
  • Zero AI-slop vocabulary (banned-list lives inside the AG1 copy-rules file - copy is run through the validator before deploy).

Avoid

  • "Get rich quick" / "make millions" - Bank of England credibility forbids it
  • US-finance vocabulary (401k, Roth, FICO)
  • Crypto-pump tone
  • Setter-call sales pages - Cazza's audience is Gen Z, prefers self-serve
  • Long-form pitch decks - the brand is short-form, the funnel must respect that
  • Anything that frames sponsorships as "selling out" - they're a layer of the stack, not a flaw

What to do instead

  • Use UK money vocabulary fluently
  • Quote real ISA / SIPP / NI numbers
  • Show the Bank of England + Global Fraud Summit credibility once, then let the offer carry the weight
  • Keep the apply-to-enrol flow under 4 minutes
  • Apply form is the CTA - no calendar, no setter
  • Show the cohort cap explicitly ("we open intakes quarterly, cap at 150")
